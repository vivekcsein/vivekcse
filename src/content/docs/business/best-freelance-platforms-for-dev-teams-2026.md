# The Best Freelance Platforms to Get Clients (2026 Guide for Developers, Studios & Small Teams)

> Written for people running a dev shop, a design studio, or a 2–10 person software/agency team — not solo hobbyists picking up gig work. If you're bidding as a "company" rather than an individual, the calculus on which platform to prioritize is different, and this guide is built around that.

---

## 1. How to think about this before picking a platform

Every platform in this space solves one of three different problems. Confusing them wastes months.

| Platform type                                                                     | What it actually does                                                                       | Fits your team when…                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Open marketplaces** (Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer.com)                            | Lets anyone post a job, anyone bid                                                          | You need volume and are willing to compete on proposals                                                                                         |
| **Vetted / curated networks** (Toptal, Arc.dev, Lemon.io, Gun.io)                 | Screens talent _before_ clients ever see a profile, then matches                            | You (or your senior devs) can pass a hard technical bar and want higher-trust, higher-rate clients without pitching                             |
| **B2B directories / review platforms** (Clutch, GoodFirms, DesignRush)            | Buyers browse ranked agency profiles with reviews — you don't bid, you get _found_          | You're positioning as a company/studio, not an individual, and want inbound leads for bigger, longer contracts                                  |
| **Zero/low-commission portfolio networks** (Contra, We Work Remotely, Braintrust) | Either a portfolio storefront with no marketplace fee, or a job board you apply to directly | You already have some audience/portfolio and want to keep more of what you bill, or you want a lead-gen channel that doesn't tax repeat clients |

A team like a small dev studio typically needs **one open marketplace for volume + one directory for inbound credibility + one niche/vetted network matching your actual stack**. Trying to be everywhere at once is how teams burn time on five half-maintained profiles instead of two strong ones.

## 2. Open marketplaces (best for volume and first clients)

### Upwork

The largest general marketplace by project volume — it has over 12 million registered freelancers and 5 million clients, and handles more than $3 billion in annual freelancer earnings.

- **Fees:** Freelancer-side commission has moved to a flat 10% for most freelancers, with a sliding scale of 0–15% on connect-driven contracts, and the fee curve drops with lifetime client spend. Client-side fees run separately, generally in the low single digits.
- **Best for:** Building the first 3–5 case studies as a company profile, ranging from short fixed-price jobs to long hourly retainers.
- **Team angle:** Upwork supports **Agency profiles**, letting you bid as "Frenzz" with a roster of members rather than one freelancer identity — use this from day one instead of an individual profile, since it lets you pool reviews and swap who's staffed on a contract.
- **Watch out for:** Account suspensions can happen and appeals are slow — agencies that recover fastest are the ones who maintained at least one backup pipeline before a suspension happened. Don't make Upwork your only channel.
- Link: https://www.upwork.com

### Freelancer.com

An open, contest- and bid-based marketplace with a very large, price-competitive global pool. It's best for price-sensitive, discrete tasks rather than long managed engagements.

- **Fees:** Commission around 10%, reportedly rising close to 20% without a paid membership tier, per third-party comparisons.
- **Best for:** Small, well-scoped fixed-price jobs (landing page builds, bug fixes, integrations) — good top-of-funnel volume, weaker for retained work.
- Link: https://www.freelancer.com

### Fiverr / Fiverr Pro

Inbound "gig" model — you list productized service packages and clients come to you, rather than you bidding on job posts.

- **Fees:** A flat 20% platform fee for standard Fiverr.
- **Best for:** Productizing something specific and repeatable (e.g. "Next.js landing page in 5 days," "React dashboard MVP"). Works well as a lead-gen channel for a defined package, not for custom scoped work.
- **Team angle:** Fiverr Pro adds a vetted tier if you want to target higher budgets without the 20% fee structure of standard Fiverr's race-to-the-bottom gigs.
- Link: https://www.fiverr.com

## 3. Vetted / curated developer networks (best once you can pass technical screening)

These platforms remove the "pitch 20 jobs to land 1" grind — you get screened once, then matched or browsed directly by clients who already trust the vetting.

### Toptal

The best-known "top 3%" network. Screening includes tests, code reviews and interviews, and rates skew premium — roughly $50–$140/hr for contract work as of 2026.

- **Fee model:** Freelancer-side fee is 0% — Toptal makes its margin by marking up the rate it quotes to the client, so a developer's effective take-home is baked into the quoted rate rather than deducted after.
- **Best for:** Senior individual contributors or a small team member going in as a specialist (full-stack, AI/ML) who wants long retainers instead of one-off gigs.
- Link: https://www.toptal.com

### Arc.dev

Remote-first network connecting developers, designers, and marketers with vetted startup/tech-company roles, combining marketplace matching with more traditional remote job listings. Best if you want consistent freelance work and are comfortable pitching clients directly once matched.

- Link: https://arc.dev

### Lemon.io

Vetted developer marketplace with notable AI specialization; commonly recommended as the more startup-budget-friendly counterpart to Toptal — Toptal screens harder and costs more, Lemon.io is the more budget-friendly option for startups.

- Link: https://lemon.io

### Gun.io

Niche-vetted network of experienced US/Canada-based contract developers, screened first by software and then personally reviewed by senior staff. Strong fit if your team can credibly claim senior-level, North-America-facing engineering work.

- Link: https://gun.io

### Braintrust

A hybrid model: it takes nothing from the freelancer's quoted rate and instead charges the client a 15% fee, with a token/governance layer on top of an otherwise conventional contract relationship. There's also a monthly platform fee while actively searching and a refundable deposit. Hourly rates commonly run $60–$150+, with highly specialized talent above $200.

- Link: https://www.usebraintrust.com

### Codeable

Specialist WordPress-only network — ideal specifically for hiring/being hired as a WordPress developer. If landing pages/WordPress sites are part of your service line, worth a dedicated profile.

- Link: https://www.codeable.io

## 4. Zero/low-commission and job-board style platforms

### Contra

Portfolio-first platform that has become known for **0% commission** — Contra charges 0% commission, the lowest in the industry, with only standard payment-processing costs passed through. It functions less like a bidding marketplace and more like a storefront you drive existing leads to.

- **Best for:** Once you have any inbound traffic (LinkedIn, referrals, past clients), Contra is a near-zero-cost way to formalize contracts and invoicing without losing a cut to commission.
- Link: https://contra.com

### We Work Remotely

A long-standing remote job board rather than a marketplace — free for job seekers to browse and apply, with no commission taken on either side. Employers pay to post; you apply directly and negotiate off-platform.

- **Best for:** Picking up longer-term remote contract roles (not one-off gigs) as a way to diversify beyond marketplace fee structures.
- Link: https://weworkremotely.com

### PeoplePerHour

UK/Europe-leaning general marketplace, comparable in structure to Upwork/Freelancer, with entry-tier fees reported around 20% before dropping with repeat-client volume.

- **Best for:** If you're specifically targeting UK/EU SMB clients, it has stronger regional density than Upwork for that segment.
- Link: https://www.peopleperhour.com

## 5. B2B directories (the channel most dev teams underuse)

This is the category that matters most once you're pitching as **"Frenzz"** rather than as an individual — these platforms don't have you bid on jobs, they let ranked, review-backed company profiles get found by buyers actively comparing agencies.

### Clutch

The default B2B review platform since 2013, with over 350,000 listed service providers and more than a million business leaders visiting monthly to find agency partners.

- **Pricing:** A basic profile is free; a Verified tier runs roughly $499/year, with sponsorship/featured placement priced separately and higher. Other estimates put premium visibility packages at roughly $1,500–$1,800+/year.
- **Reality check:** Traffic has reportedly been declining and the platform leans hard into paid sponsorship, so reviewers are increasingly questioning whether premium placement still justifies the cost — but the free listing alone still carries credibility value, and the phone-verification process for reviews is considered best-in-class.
- **Team angle:** Claim a free profile early, collect a few verified client reviews (Clutch calls and verifies clients directly), and treat paid tiers as optional once you can measure lead quality.
- Link: https://clutch.co

### GoodFirms

Second-tier alternative that many agencies run _alongside_ Clutch rather than instead of it. It evaluates and ranks over 60,000 listed software firms using market presence, client feedback and portfolio depth rather than ad spend alone, with 795,000+ monthly visitors.

- **Pricing:** Free-forever listing subject to a four-step research verification; a Pro tier runs about $49/month ($588/year) for featured placement and expanded review tools.
- **Note:** Approval isn't automatic — GoodFirms' research team reviews your portfolio, checks the company, and contacts client references, so prepare a consistent website, a real company email, named leadership, and clients willing to verify.
- Link: https://www.goodfirms.co

### The Manifest / DesignRush / Sortlist

Supplementary directories worth a low-effort profile:

- **The Manifest** shares Clutch's underlying data — providers are told to create or claim a Clutch profile, and eligible profiles are then automatically featured on The Manifest too, so it's essentially free extra reach off one Clutch listing.
- **DesignRush / Sortlist** skew more toward marketing, web design, and creative categories if Frenzz's landing-page/website work is the lead offering rather than pure engineering.

## 7. A practical stack for a small dev/agency team

Rather than joining everything, most small teams get the best ROI from a **3-lane setup**:

1. **Volume lane — Upwork (Agency profile).** Bid actively for the first 90 days to build 3–5 strong case studies and reviews. Treat this as lead-gen infrastructure, not a permanent home.
2. **Inbound credibility lane — Clutch + GoodFirms.** Free tiers first. Ask every satisfied client for a verified review — this is what actually moves you up in directory rankings and is worth more long-term than paid placement.
3. **Fit-specific lane — pick one:**
   - If you have senior full-stack/AI engineers who can pass hard screening → **Toptal or Gun.io**.
   - If your work is more productized (landing pages, MVPs, WordPress) → **Contra** (to convert existing leads at 0% fee) + **Codeable** if WordPress is a real line of business.

A useful discipline mentioned across multiple 2026 comparisons: platforms are lead-generation tools, not the business itself — the actual long-term win is converting marketplace clients into direct, repeat relationships once trust is established, which lowers your effective fee over time on repeat work.

## 8. Checklist before you post your first profile

- [ ] Register as a **company/agency profile** wherever the platform supports it (Upwork, Clutch, GoodFirms) — not an individual freelancer profile — since you're pitching Frenzz's team capacity, not one person's time.
- [ ] Use consistent handles/branding across profiles (matches your `frenzzofficial` / `company/frenzz` handles) so buyers researching you across directories see one consistent identity.
- [ ] Prepare 2–3 case studies with concrete outcomes (load times, conversion lift, integration scope) rather than just screenshots — directories like Clutch/GoodFirms weight verified outcomes heavily.
- [ ] Decide your **productized offer** (e.g., "AI-powered landing page in 7 days") before writing Fiverr/Contra listings — vague generalist listings underperform specific ones.
- [ ] Set a review cadence: ask every completed client for a review on at least one open marketplace and one directory, since reviews compound your ranking over time.
- [ ] Budget a backup channel (per Upwork's suspension risk noted above) so one platform's policy change can't stall the whole pipeline.
